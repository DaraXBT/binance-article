# screen-print

Bold poster rendering built from separated flat inks, geometric silhouettes, and restrained analog texture.

## Core Characteristics

Use a limited set of solid color plates with hard-edged shapes. The result should resemble a collectible screen-printed poster, never a digitally painted or photorealistic image.

## Lines and Shapes

- Bold geometric contours and readable silhouettes
- Flat color blocks with no gradients or inner shading
- Optional slight color-registration offset for analog character
- Symbolic forms favored over literal detail

## Texture

- Restrained halftone dots and subtle paper grain
- Texture supports depth but never reduces title or silhouette legibility
- No glossy effects, lens blur, or photographic noise

## Depth

- Build depth with overlap, scale, and foreground/background ink values
- Keep one consistent horizon or directional flow
- Use dark negative space as an active compositional layer

## Element Vocabulary

Silhouette figures, architectural fragments, symbolic objects, geometric infrastructure, stars, rays, halftone fields, and transformation motifs such as cracking, dissolving, or reassembly.

## Typography Approach

Bold condensed sans-serif or display lettering with strong poster hierarchy. Keep copy short and place it in a clean ink field; do not texture letter interiors enough to hurt readability.

## Best For

Panoramic narratives, before/after stories, cultural or technology evolution, cinematic covers, and limited-edition poster aesthetics.
